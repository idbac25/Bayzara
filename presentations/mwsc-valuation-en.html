Part 1 · North America

Independent appraisal is the law.

Many US states require that a water system be independently appraised before it is sold — specifically to protect the seller from being underpaid.

How the law works
  • Each side hires its own independent valuation expert.
  • Every expert must use three methods: cost, income, and market.
  • The price is the average of the appraisals.
What it produces
1.7×
Aquarion Water sold for $2.4 billion — 1.7 times its asset base (2025).
US deals run $6,000–$11,000 per connection.

Lesson  Serious markets make independent valuation mandatory. The seller always brings their own expert.

Sources: Pennsylvania Act 12 / Section 1329; NARUC 2021; Eversource–Aquarion 2025.

Part 1 · Africa

Two ways this ends — lose or keep your value.

The losing path

Book-value absorption

In Kenya, South Africa & Tanzania, when a big public utility absorbs a small one, assets transfer at book value — no premium, nothing extra for the smaller partner.

The winning path

Keep the asset, earn from it

In Senegal & Côte d'Ivoire, the original owner keeps ownership of the wells and network and is paid to let others operate — value retained, not surrendered.

The danger  If MWSC lets Banadir frame this as a “book-value absorption,” you give away the aquifer, the territory, and the future for almost nothing.

Sources: WASREB Kenya; SA Water Services Act mergers; Senegal SONES/SEN'EAU; Côte d'Ivoire SODECI.

Part 2 · The crown jewel

You don't own pipes. You own access to the only good water a city of millions can drink.

Your wellfields · the Shabelle aquifer
  • Fresh water — salinity under 1,000 mg/l, good to drink.
  • High yield — 12–20 litres/second per well.
  • Sited 25–30 km inland on purpose, on the river's fresh aquifer.
The city's own wells
  • Salty — ruined by seawater intrusion near the coast.
  • Weak — only 1–5 litres/second.
  • The reason the whole system was built away from the city.

This is the point  Anyone can drill a salty borehole in town. Almost no one can replace your access to the Shabelle fresh-water aquifer. That scarcity is your real value.

Source: British Geological Survey, Hydrogeology of Somalia; Faillace 1986; RVI 2022.

Why “output” is a trap

  • Today's output is low because of war damage — not because the asset is small.
  • It values you at your weakest moment, ignoring 48,500 m³/day of design capacity.
  • It pays nothing for the aquifer access, the territory, or what it would cost to rebuild this today.

Asset vs Output  Output is the tip above the water. The asset is the mountain beneath it.
